How Rivell reviews a contract.

Three engines share one knowledge base, and a person signs off between them.

Start here

Select any part of the system.

Dark boxes are the engines. Dashed boxes are where a person decides. The band below holds the four kinds of intelligence the engines share.

  • Input

    Your knowledge

    The playbooks, clause libraries, templates and past deals your team already keeps, in Word or Excel.

  • Engine

    Knowledge engine

    Reads each document with its headings, tables and rows intact. Tags every entry with its topic and the roles it states, such as preferred, fallback, walk-away or escalation. Flags entries that duplicate, contradict or replace what is already published.

  • You

    You approve every entry

    Nothing reaches a review until a person has checked it against its source and published it.

  • Store

    Published knowledge

    Versioned. Every review records the exact version it used, so a past review can always be explained.

  • Input

    Incoming contract

    A Word document on either side's paper. A file that already carries tracked changes is reviewed as if they were accepted. Settle them in Word before Rivell writes its redline.

  • Engine

    Review engine

    Reads the contract's structure, parties and your side. Gathers the positions that apply, judges each clause, checks across clauses, and turns the results into findings with a fixed severity.

  • You

    You decide the findings

    Accept, edit, keep as drafted, dismiss or escalate. Findings marked informational need no decision. You can also add a finding Rivell did not raise.

  • Engine

    Redline engine

    Turns each decision into an exact replace, insert or delete on the original document, written as a tracked change.

  • Output

    Redlined Word file

    Read back after it is written. Each change is checked against what you approved before the file is offered for download.

  • Intelligence

    Rules

    Fixed logic owns structure, the order of authority, severity and the final check of the file. Used by all three engines. The rules always give the same answer for the same input.

  • Intelligence

    Jev, a System One decision model by TypeSafe

    Answers fixed-choice questions about each entry and each clause, and says how likely each answer is. Used by the knowledge and review engines.

  • Intelligence

    Language model

    Turns free-form text into structured positions, drafts wording when nothing in your library fits, and gives a second opinion on close calls. Used by the knowledge and review engines.

  • Intelligence

    Hybrid search

    Searches by exact words and by meaning, then combines the results, so it finds the positions a clause touches even in different words. Meaning-based search needs a model you set up. Used by the review engine.

Applied · Verified

  1. Rivell finds the clause in the document's structure and places it under a topic: liability cap. It already knows your side of the deal. You are the supplier.

  2. It collects what applies, in order of authority: your playbook position first, then approved wording, then similar clauses from past deals. Search can surface a source. It can never promote one.

  3. Jev compares the clause with the positions that apply, all at once. It does not write an essay. It picks an answer and says how likely each option is.

  4. Rules look at how sure Jev is. A clear answer stands, so a clear breach becomes a finding. A close call goes to the language model for a second opinion. If the two disagree, the clause is marked for your look.

  5. Rivell checks that your approved wording meets the position and closes this gap. Here it does. When nothing in your library fits, Rivell drafts new wording and labels it Proposed by Rivell.

  6. You see the gap, the wording and who must approve. Accept, edit, keep the clause, or escalate it.

  7. Your accepted change becomes a tracked change in the original Word file. Rivell reads the file back and confirms the change is exactly what you approved.

What each part does.

Rules

Fixed logic, no model

Owns the document structure, which source outranks which, how severe a finding is, and the final check of the Word file. Legal work needs the same answer for the same input, so severity never depends on how confident a model feels.

Jev

System One decision model by TypeSafe

Answers fixed-choice questions: yes or no, pick one, or rate. It answers all of a clause's questions at once and gives a likelihood for every answer. Its judgments are fast and consistent, and Rivell can see when it is unsure.

Language model

Your choice of provider

Turns free-form playbook text into structured positions, drafts wording when nothing in your library fits, gives second opinions on close calls, and reads the whole contract for issues your playbook does not cover. Some questions need language, not a choice.

Hybrid search

Exact words and meaning

Searches by exact words and by meaning at the same time, then merges the two result lists. Exact words miss a paraphrase, and meaning alone can miss a defined term, so together they find the position a clause touches. Meaning-based search runs once you choose a model for it.

You

The final word

You approve the knowledge, decide each finding that needs a call, and add what Rivell missed. Rivell assists. A lawyer signs off.

Why a decision model sits at the centre.

Most AI tools ask a language model to write an answer, then read a decision out of its prose. Rivell asks most of its questions of a System One decision model instead.

TypeSafe borrowed the name from System 1 in psychology, the fast and intuitive mode of thinking. The model answers many structured questions at once and gives a likelihood for every option, so rules can act on its doubt.

Authority comes before similarity.

Search finds clauses that look alike, but it cannot know which ones your team agreed to. Every source has a fixed rank, so a close match can inform a finding but never override your playbook.

Built to be checked.

Every review can be explained

A review keeps the contract version and the knowledge version it used, so you can always see why it said what it said.

Changes are read back and checked

After it writes tracked changes, Rivell reads the Word file back and checks each one. A change it cannot place exactly is reported, never guessed.

What Rivell is built with.

Self-hosted. Contracts and knowledge stay on your own servers. Clause text goes only to the model providers you configure.
