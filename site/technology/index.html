How Rivell reviews a contract.

Rivell is not one model with a long prompt. It is a hybrid: a System-1 decision model, a language model, hybrid search and plain code, each given the job it does best. Here is how they work together.

Three engines. One shared knowledge base.

The knowledge engine turns your documents into positions. The review engine judges every contract against them. The redline engine writes the result into Word.

Dark boxes are the engines. Dashed boxes are where a person decides. The band below holds the four kinds of intelligence the engines share.

  • Input

    Your knowledge

    The playbooks, clause libraries, templates and past deals your team already keeps, in Word or Excel.

  • Engine

    Knowledge engine

    Reads each document with its headings, tables and rows intact. Sorts every entry by topic and role: preferred, fallback, walk-away or escalation. Flags entries that duplicate or contradict what is already published.

  • You

    You approve every entry

    Nothing reaches a review until a person has checked it against its source and published it.

  • Store

    Published knowledge

    Versioned. Every review records the exact version it used, so a past review can always be explained.

  • Input

    Incoming contract

    A Word document on either side's paper, including one that already carries the other side's tracked changes and comments.

  • Engine

    Review engine

    Reads the contract's structure, parties and your side. Gathers the positions that apply, judges each clause, checks across clauses, and turns the results into findings with a fixed severity.

  • You

    You decide every finding

    Accept, edit, keep as drafted, dismiss or escalate. You can also add a finding Rivell did not raise.

  • Engine

    Redline engine

    Turns each decision into an exact replace, insert or delete on the original document, written as a tracked change.

  • Output

    Redlined Word file

    Read back after it is written. Each change is checked against what you approved before the file is offered for download.

  • Intelligence

    Rules

    Plain code owns structure, the order of authority, severity and the final check of the file. Used by all three engines. The same input always gives the same answer.

  • Intelligence

    Jev, a System-1 decision model by TypeSafe

    Answers bounded questions about each entry and each clause, and reports how likely every answer is. Used by the knowledge and review engines.

  • Intelligence

    Language model

    Reads free-form text into fields, drafts wording when your library has none, and gives a second opinion on close calls. Used by the knowledge and review engines.

  • Intelligence

    Hybrid search

    Full-text search and meaning-based search, ranked together, to find the positions a clause touches in different words. Used by the review engine.

Verified · matches your decision

  1. Rivell finds the clause in the document's structure and places it under a topic: liability cap. It already knows your side of the deal. You are the supplier.

  2. It collects what applies, in order of authority: your playbook position first, then approved wording, then similar clauses from past deals. Search can surface a source. It can never promote one.

  3. Jev compares the clause with every position that applies, in a single call. It does not write an essay. It picks an answer and says how likely each option is.

  4. Rules read the spread. A clear answer becomes a finding. A close call goes to the language model for a second opinion. If the two disagree, the clause is marked for your look.

  5. Rivell checks whether your approved wording meets the position and fixes this gap. Here it does. When nothing fits, Rivell drafts new wording and labels it as drafted.

  6. You see the gap, the wording and who must approve. Accept, edit, keep the clause, or escalate it.

  7. Your accepted change becomes a tracked change in the original Word file. Rivell reads the file back and confirms the change is exactly what you approved.

Every part has one job.

Rules

Plain code

Does

Owns the document structure, which source outranks which, how severe a finding is, and the final check of the Word file.

Why it is here

Legal work needs the same answer for the same input. Severity never depends on how confident a model feels.

Jev

System-1 decision model by TypeSafe

Does

Answers bounded questions: yes or no, choose one, rate. All of a clause's questions go out in one call, and every answer carries a likelihood.

Why it is here

Fast, consistent judgments, and a measure of doubt that code can act on.

Language model

Your choice of provider

Does

Reads free-form playbook text into fields, drafts wording when your library has none, gives second opinions, and reads the whole contract for issues your playbook does not cover.

Why it is here

Some questions need language, not a choice.

Hybrid search

Full text and meaning

Does

Runs keyword search and meaning-based search side by side, then merges their rankings into one list.

Why it is here

Keywords miss a paraphrase. Meaning alone misses an exact term. Together they find the position a clause touches.

You

The final word

Does

Why a decision model sits at the centre.

Most AI tools ask a language model to write an answer, then try to read a decision out of its prose. Rivell asks most of its questions of a System-1 decision model instead.

The name comes from the fast, intuitive mode of thinking described in psychology. A System-1 model answers structured questions directly, many at once, and gives a likelihood for every option. Each judgment is quick and consistent. It also gives the review something prose cannot: a measure of doubt that rules can act on.

Authority comes before similarity.

Search is good at finding things that look alike. It cannot know which of them your team agreed to. Rivell keeps the two apart. Every source has a fixed rank, and a close match from a lower rank can inform a finding but never override a higher one.

When no position in your playbook applies, Rivell says so. It never fills the gap with a clause from a past deal.

Built to be checked.

Every review can be traced.

A review records the knowledge version, the review pack and the engine version it used, so you can always see why it said what it said.

Every change is verified.

After it writes tracked changes, Rivell reads the Word file back and checks each one. A change it cannot place exactly is reported, never guessed.

Every release is measured.

Engine changes are tested on contracts kept apart from development, and compared with a plain language-model baseline before they ship.
